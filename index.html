Why do bank customers leave?

A churn analysis of 10,000 bank customers using MySQL and Power BI. It finds who is leaving, where, and what the bank can do about it.

Key findings

Churn is not random. It clusters in a few places, which means the bank can target its retention effort.

32.44%

Germany churns twice as fast

France and Spain are near 16%. Germany also holds 52.8% of the balance lost to exits.

7.58%

Two products keep customers

Customers with two products rarely leave. Single-product customers churn at 27.71%.

56.04%

Age 50–59 is the danger zone

Under 40, churn stays below 11%. It climbs sharply after 40.

26.85%

Inactive members leave more

Active members churn at 14.27%, so engagement matters more than tenure.

25.07%

Women churn more than men

Female customers churn at 25.07% against 16.46% for male customers.

0.6 pts

Credit card, salary and score barely matter

Card holders churn at 20.18% and non-holders at 20.81%.

Recommendations

Each action is tied directly to a finding in the data.

  1. Re-engage inactive membersInactive customers churn at 26.85%, against 14.27% for active ones.
  2. Run a Germany retention planGermany churns at 32.44% and holds the most money at risk.
  3. Build two-product relationshipsTwo products cut churn to 7.58%. Review why 3–4 products churn 83–100%.
  4. Protect customers aged 40–60Churn reaches 30.79% at 40–49 and 56.04% at 50–59: offer personal service.
  5. Work from the risk score weeklyContact High-risk customers first, using the dashboard risk table.

About the project

Seven tables (two core customer tables and five lookups) were cleaned in Power Query, analysed with MySQL to answer 26 business questions, and visualised in Power BI. Data checks found no missing values or duplicates.
